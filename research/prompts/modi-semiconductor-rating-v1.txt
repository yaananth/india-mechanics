India Mechanics semiconductor evidence rating prompt, version 1

Subject: Narendra Modi as Prime Minister of India, 2014-present.

Use the same six components and published cross-PM standard:

- observed outcomes;
- durable reforms;
- inclusion;
- crisis and security;
- institutions and liberties;
- integrity and execution.

Current component scores before this review:

- outcomes 7.7;
- reforms 7.4;
- inclusion 6.5;
- crisis 6.4;
- institutions 4.5;
- integrity and execution 5.9.

New evidence to assess:

1. The India Semiconductor Mission was approved in December 2021 with a
   Rs 76,000 crore incentive framework.
2. By July 2026, twelve approved manufacturing projects represented about
   Rs 1.64 lakh crore of project investment and three plants had entered
   commercial production.
3. Micron Sanand and CG Semi are real commercial assembly, packaging, and test
   production. They are not front-end wafer fabrication.
4. Tata Dholera and the high-volume Tata Assam facility remain under
   construction. Planned capacity, projected jobs, local value, yields,
   customers, and exports are not completed outcomes.
5. Semicon 2.0 was approved on July 15, 2026 with a separate
   Rs 1,27,500 crore six-year programme envelope.
6. The Taloja 80,000-wafers-per-month and Assam 48-million-chips-per-day
   figures are planned capacity, not current production.
7. Fairchild seriously considered India in the mid-1960s, but the precise
   Robert Noyce visit, complete-blueprint, and fifty-office details in the viral
   narrative are not established by the reviewed historical record.
8. SCL created real public semiconductor capability; its 1989 fire and slow
   recovery were severe.
9. UPA-era Intel policy delay was a real execution failure. The 2007 policy and
   2014 commercial-fab approvals also existed but did not produce an operating
   fab.

Rules:

- Credit verified plant delivery and durable policy architecture.
- Do not count project approval, planned capacity, projected jobs, or a newly
  approved programme as observed outcomes.
- Do not change unrelated inclusion, crisis, or institutional scores without
  component-specific evidence.
- Do not count semiconductor capability in both the main crisis score and a
  separate security specialist score.
- Preserve earlier-government credit and failures under the same standard.
- Return a generic rating, recommended component scores, a balanced rating
  using weights 30%, 20%, 15%, 10%, 15%, and 10%, and a standardized rating
  using weights 25%, 20%, 15%, 15%, 15%, and 10%.
